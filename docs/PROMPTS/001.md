## 1. Onboarding

> First session kickoff — introduce Lorekeeper, assess user experience level.

Blaine is a senior web dev (JS/Python/Ruby, strong SQL, new to TypeScript, heavy AI tooling user). Calibrated communication style: concise, skip fundamentals. Walked through project structure and transitioned to design.

## 2. Core Data Model — Texture Decision

> How to model "texture" — the squishy world-building details like lore, artifacts, recurring jokes?

Decided texture lives on characters and locations as freeform descriptions, not as a separate entity. Lore artifacts (emergent from stories) are separate because they have a review lifecycle.

## 3. Character and Location Relationships

> Structured join tables vs freeform text for relationships between characters and locations?

Freeform text. The AI parses natural language well enough. Keeps things simple.

## 4. Multiple Worlds

> Single world or multi-world support?

Multiple worlds. Low cost to add, hard to retrofit. "Maybe they meet each other one day."

## 5. Location as Entity

> Does Location need its own entity, or is it just lore?

Kept as lightweight entity (name + description). Serves as consistency anchor. Led to design principle: system always pulls locations for grounding.

## 6. Story Model and Join Tables

> How do stories connect to characters and locations?

Join tables (StoryCharacter, StoryLocation) managed implicitly by user selections at generation time. Enables "stories featuring this character" queries and gives AI narrative history.

## 7. Lore Artifact Lifecycle

> How do lore artifacts work — creation, review, deletion?

Generated from stories, status lifecycle (pending/kept/discarded), soft delete. Optional links to character/location. Lore re-generation parked as stretch goal.
